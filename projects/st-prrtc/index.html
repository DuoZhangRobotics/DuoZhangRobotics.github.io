Abstract

ST-pRRTC is a GPU-parallel space-time RRT-Connect planner for known obstacle trajectories and an unspecified arrival time. It grows a shared forward tree and a backward forest rooted at candidate goal-arrival times. Searching more arrival times broadens coverage but divides a finite planning budget among more trees. We study two ways to allocate that search effort: interval root samples goal times continuously, while root recycling reuses a bounded set of tree slots after finding an earlier solution. Interval root has probabilistic completeness and asymptotic arrival-time optimality for robust solutions in a bounded horizon. Root recycling is a practical policy without those general guarantees. Across three dynamic benchmarks, both variants achieve lower mean first-solution compute times and earlier mean final arrivals than ST-RRT* and SI-RRT on cases solved by all compared methods. A UR5e executing plans among moving Crazyflie drones demonstrates the approach on hardware.

ST-pRRTC: interval root and root recycling

A backward tree needs both a goal configuration and a goal-arrival time. GPU workers expand a shared forward tree and the backward forest in parallel, checking edges against moving obstacles at their corresponding times.

Interval root

Uniform sampling preserves coverage of candidate arrival times. Goal collision checks suggest guided sampling windows, and the planner collision-checks every sampled root. An earlier solution lowers the arrival bound while preserving useful search trees.

Guarantees: for a single goal in a bounded horizon, interval root is probabilistically complete for positive-clearance solutions and asymptotically optimal in arrival time when robust solutions approach the optimum. The analysis assumes unlimited sampling and storage, fair worker scheduling, and sound local collision validation that accepts sufficiently short collision-free motions.

Root recycling

Root recycling uses a fixed set of backward-tree slots. After an earlier solution, it replaces later roots with new, empty roots at earlier times and retains the shared forward tree and useful earlier backward trees. Recycled roots do not inherit the old tree's nodes. This policy focuses finite search effort on useful arrival times and does not inherit interval root's general completeness or optimality guarantees.

Benchmarks

We compare root recycling and interval root with ST-RRT* and SI-RRT on Disc2D, a Panda arm among moving spheres, and UR5e tabletop transitions. In the tabletop benchmark, one arm plans against the prescribed motion of the other. Blue denotes the planned robot and red the moving obstacles. Translucent arms show goal configurations. The tabletop serves as visual context.

Top plots show cumulative first-solution success over all cases, averaged across planning budgets of 0.5, 1, 3, and 5 seconds. Their shading spans the minimum and maximum across budgets. Bottom plots show mean final arrival and 95% bootstrap intervals on cases solved by all four methods at each budget.

Compute time measures GPU-kernel execution for ST-pRRTC and CPU planning calls for the baselines, excluding scene construction and upload. Experiments use an RTX 4090 and an Intel Core i9-14900KF, with single-threaded CPU baselines.

Real-robot demonstrations
